How do I know you can't see my key?

Don't take our word for it — this app makes two kinds of network request, and you can watch the difference live: requests to Quindral's own server (routing decisions, feedback, pricing lookups), and requests directly from this browser tab to your model provider (e.g. api.openai.com) using your saved key. Your key is only ever used in the second kind — it never touches Quindral's server, in any request, full stop.

What Quindral's server does see: your prompt text, and the model's response text, are sent to and stored on our server when you rate a response or it gets logged — this is real content retention, used to evaluate model quality over time (see the Feedback page), not just routing metadata. This is a deliberate tradeoff, not an accident, and it's new: your key never leaves the browser, but as of this build, response content now does. If that's not something you want, don't rate responses or run real queries here yet.

Every request this page has made this session is logged below, in order, labeled by which kind it is and what it carries. You can verify this independently any time with your browser's DevTools → Network tab; this list is just the same information without needing DevTools.

Why we evaluate model responses

Routing to "the best model" only means something if you can also tell when a model got it wrong. Here's a real example from testing this app, not a hypothetical.

Prompt: "Which female sang on The Smiths' Bigmouth Strikes Again?"

gpt-4o-mini — judged FAIL
The female vocal part was sung by Nena Hawkes.

Fabricated — there is no "Nena Hawkes" connected to this song. A confident, fluent, completely made-up name: exactly the kind of error that's easy to miss if you only ever see one model's answer.

gpt-5.4 — judged PASS
Despite what the liner notes might say, no female singer actually sang on the track — the high-pitched backing vocals were Morrissey's own voice, pitch-shifted.

Correct and appropriately nuanced.

Both responses came from models this app can route to. Left to chance, a user could just as easily have landed on the wrong one with no way to know. That gap is what the offline evaluator exists to close: every real response gets judged after the fact by a separate model, pass/fail plus a reason, and a model's track record — on this specific route, not just in general — feeds back into future routing decisions as its own signal, alongside real human thumbs-down votes. See the Feedback log for what's actually been recorded.

EU AI Act

This is a description of how the app is built, not a legal compliance certification — we're not a law firm and this isn't legal advice. Worth reading as "what we've done," not "we are certified compliant."

  • Transparency by design. The Act's Article 50 requires that people know when they're interacting with an AI system and, for certain uses, understand how automated decisions are made. This app's entire premise is showing the routing decision — which model, why, what got excluded and why, what it cost — rather than a black-box answer.
  • Human oversight, not full automation. Routing decisions are suggestions you can override (run a different eligible model, vote a decision down), and the response-quality signal blends real human feedback with automated judging rather than either one alone silently deciding what happens next.
  • Data minimization where it's free to do. Your API key never reaches this server at all, in either direction (see above). Where content is retained (prompts/responses, for quality evaluation), that retention is disclosed live in-app, not buried in a policy document — see the network log on the Settings page.
  • EU-only hard filter. A user-controlled toggle restricts routing to EU-hosted models — relevant to data residency requirements some EU users/orgs may separately have, independent of the AI Act itself.

Not yet addressed: a formal risk-tier self-assessment under the Act's classification system, a published data retention/deletion policy, and (for redistribution rather than personal/demo use) whatever obligations would apply to whoever operates or ships this app as a service, not just how it's built. Flag if this needs to go further before real EU users rely on it.
